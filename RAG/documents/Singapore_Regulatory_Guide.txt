SINGAPORE BUSINESS EXPANSION REGULATORY KNOWLEDGE BASE
===========================================================

PURPOSE
-------
This is a curated knowledge base for an AI Business Expansion Regulatory
Advisor helping startups evaluate expansion into Singapore.

IMPORTANT:
- This is informational and is NOT legal, tax, immigration, accounting,
  or professional advice.
- Regulations, fees, salary thresholds, quotas, rebates and procedures can
  change. Always verify important decisions against the latest official
  government source.
- Current information should be treated as a snapshot checked in August 2026.
- Each section preserves an official source URL for traceability.

===========================================================
1. COUNTRY AND FOREIGN BUSINESS ENTRY
===========================================================

Singapore offers several routes for foreign businesses to establish a
presence. A foreign business may consider:
1. Representative office
2. Singapore subsidiary/local company
3. Foreign company branch
4. Re-domiciliation

Foreign businesses must engage a Corporate Service Provider (CSP) to register
their business in Singapore.

Foreign founders must also consider local residency requirements. If a
foreign founder intends to move to Singapore to operate the business, an
appropriate work pass is required.

OFFICIAL SOURCES:
ACRA - Setting up a foreign business:
https://www.acra.gov.sg/register/foreign-business/

ACRA - Ways to set up foreign businesses:
https://www.acra.gov.sg/register/foreign-business/ways-to-set-up/

===========================================================
2. BUSINESS REGISTRATION
===========================================================

A business generally needs to be registered when it carries out an activity
for profit on a regular basis, unless an exemption applies. Registration is
also generally required when a business operates under a name other than the
owner's full legal name.

ACRA's business registration process is organized into five broad stages:
1. Understand requirements and eligibility.
2. Choose a business structure.
3. Choose and reserve a business name.
4. Register the business structure through Bizfile.
5. Complete post-registration requirements.

A person generally must be at least 18 years old to register a business.

OFFICIAL SOURCES:
https://www.acra.gov.sg/register/business/
https://www.acra.gov.sg/register/business/requirements-eligibility/

===========================================================
3. FOREIGNER REQUIREMENTS
===========================================================

Foreigners must engage a Corporate Service Provider (CSP) to reserve a name
and register a business structure.

Every business must have at least one local resident in Singapore. The exact
role of the local resident depends on the business structure.

ACRA identifies eligible local-resident categories including:
- Singapore citizens
- Singapore permanent residents
- Certain eligible pass holders, including Employment Pass holders,
  Personalised Employment Pass holders and Overseas Networks & Expertise
  Pass holders.

Foreign Identification Number (FIN) holders should check with their pass
issuer before registering a business name or accepting a role.

If a foreign founder wants to move to Singapore to run the business, the
founder needs an appropriate work pass.

For entrepreneurs with venture-backed or innovative technology companies,
EntrePass may be a possible route subject to eligibility requirements.

OFFICIAL SOURCE:
https://www.acra.gov.sg/register/business/requirements-eligibility/

===========================================================
4. WAYS TO SET UP A FOREIGN BUSINESS
===========================================================

4.1 REPRESENTATIVE OFFICE
-------------------------
A representative office is intended for market research before committing
to a Singapore setup.

It is temporary, is not a separate legal entity, and the parent company
remains responsible. It cannot earn income.

4.2 SUBSIDIARY / LOCAL COMPANY
------------------------------
A subsidiary is a separate Singapore company owned by the foreign parent.

It has separate legal status and liability is generally limited to the
subsidiary's assets. It can conduct commercial operations, earn income and
is subject to corporate taxation.

4.3 FOREIGN COMPANY BRANCH
--------------------------
A branch is a direct extension of the foreign parent company.

It is not a separate legal entity. The parent company remains responsible
for the branch.

A foreign company branch:
- can earn income;
- requires a locally resident authorised representative;
- has statutory and disclosure requirements;
- has annual filing obligations;
- requires business-name reservation before registration;
- requires a Corporate Service Provider for registration.

4.4 RE-DOMICILIATION
--------------------
Re-domiciliation allows an eligible foreign company to transfer its legal
registration to Singapore. The foreign company becomes a Singapore company
and must comply with Singapore requirements after re-domiciliation.

OFFICIAL SOURCE:
https://www.acra.gov.sg/register/foreign-business/ways-to-set-up/

===========================================================
5. BUSINESS STRUCTURES
===========================================================

Singapore provides several business structures:
- Sole proprietorship
- Partnership
- Limited Partnership (LP)
- Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)
- Company

5.1 SOLE PROPRIETORSHIP
-----------------------
This is the simplest business form with one owner.

It is not a separate legal entity. The owner has unlimited liability for
business debts and losses.

ACRA currently lists a one-year registration fee of S$115, consisting of a
S$15 name application fee and S$100 registration fee.

5.2 PARTNERSHIP
---------------
A partnership is operated by at least two partners.

It is not a separate legal entity and partners generally have unlimited
personal liability for partnership debts.

5.3 LIMITED PARTNERSHIP
-----------------------
An LP has at least one general partner and at least one limited partner.

The general partner has unlimited liability and manages the LP. A limited
partner's liability is generally limited to the agreed contribution, but a
limited partner should not manage the LP or may be treated as a general
partner.

If all general partners are foreigners, a local manager meeting the
requirements must be appointed.

5.4 LIMITED LIABILITY PARTNERSHIP
---------------------------------
An LLP is a separate legal entity from its partners.

It combines partnership flexibility with limited liability protection.

An LLP can own property, enter contracts, sue and be sued in its own name.

An LLP must have at least one manager meeting local residency requirements.

Key ongoing requirements include maintaining records and registers and
filing annual declarations.

5.5 PRIVATE LIMITED COMPANY
---------------------------
A company is a separate legal entity.

Shareholders generally have limited liability. A company must have at least
one shareholder and at least one director.

Ongoing requirements can include:
- company secretary appointment where required;
- auditor appointment unless exempt;
- company registers;
- annual general meeting requirements;
- annual returns.

ACRA currently lists S$315 as the basic name application plus incorporation
fee for a company: S$15 name application + S$300 incorporation.

ACRA identifies private company forms such as Pte Ltd as common structures
for startups and small businesses.

OFFICIAL SOURCE:
https://www.acra.gov.sg/register/business/choosing-business-structure/

===========================================================
6. COMPANY CONSTITUTION
===========================================================

A company constitution sets the rules for operating a company or subsidiary.

It defines rights and responsibilities of directors, shareholders and company
secretaries.

It may include:
- company name;
- business activities;
- operating rules;
- shareholder liability;
- share capital;
- issued shares;
- registered office address.

The registered office is where legal notices can be sent and company records
and registers are maintained.

ACRA provides a model constitution option.

OFFICIAL SOURCE:
https://www.acra.gov.sg/register/business/registering-different-business-structures/local-company/company-constitution/

===========================================================
7. FOREIGN COMPANY BRANCH REGISTRATION
===========================================================

A foreign company can register a branch in Singapore.

Important requirements include:
- locally resident authorised representative;
- statutory and disclosure compliance;
- annual filing requirements;
- approved business name;
- engagement of a CSP;
- foreign company incorporation/registration information;
- foreign registration number;
- position-holder information;
- controller information;
- required supporting documents.

The branch registration is performed through Bizfile.

OFFICIAL SOURCE:
https://www.acra.gov.sg/register/foreign-business/foreign-company-branch/

===========================================================
8. POST-REGISTRATION AND ANNUAL COMPLIANCE
===========================================================

Foreign companies have ongoing compliance requirements after registration.

ACRA provides a post-registration checklist covering immediate next steps,
requirements before operations and optional business services.

Foreign companies must file financial statements for both their head office
and Singapore branch operations each year, subject to applicable rules and
exemptions.

OFFICIAL SOURCES:
https://www.acra.gov.sg/register/business/after-registering/foreign-company/

https://www.acra.gov.sg/manage/companies/legal-requirements-common-offences/annual-filing-for-foreign-companies/

===========================================================
9. REGISTER OF REGISTRABLE CONTROLLERS
===========================================================

Singapore maintains a Central Register of Registrable Controllers (Central
RORC).

Unless exempt, companies including foreign companies and LLPs must file
information about registrable controllers with ACRA.

The register concerns people who beneficially own or control the entity.

OFFICIAL SOURCE:
https://www.acra.gov.sg/manage/companies/legal-requirements-common-offences/maintaining-local-companys-information-registers/rorc/central-rorc/

===========================================================
10. CORPORATE TAXATION
===========================================================

Singapore's Corporate Income Tax is administered by IRAS.

The current corporate income tax rate is 17% of chargeable income.

The 17% rate applies to both local and foreign companies.

Corporate Income Tax is assessed on a preceding-year basis.

OFFICIAL SOURCES:
https://www.iras.gov.sg/quick-links/tax-rates/corporate-income-tax-rates

https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/basics-of-corporate-income-tax/basic-guide-to-corporate-income-tax-for-companies/

10.1 NEW COMPANY TAX EXEMPTION
------------------------------
Qualifying new companies can receive tax exemptions during their first
three consecutive Years of Assessment, subject to the applicable conditions.

For the current relevant scheme, IRAS lists:
- 75% exemption on the first S$100,000 of normal chargeable income;
- 50% exemption on the next S$100,000;
- maximum exemption of S$125,000 per YA under that scheme.

Eligibility and the applicable Year of Assessment must be checked before
claiming the exemption.

10.2 YA 2026 TAX REBATE
-----------------------
IRAS states that for YA 2026 a 40% Corporate Income Tax rebate of corporate
tax payable is granted to taxpaying companies, whether tax resident or not,
under the published Budget 2026 rules.

This is a time-sensitive rebate and should NOT be treated as a permanent
tax rate or permanent exemption.

OFFICIAL SOURCE:
https://www.iras.gov.sg/quick-links/tax-rates/corporate-income-tax-rates

11. FOREIGN WORKERS AND LABOR
===========================================================

Startups expanding to Singapore may need work passes when hiring foreign
employees.

Important passes include:
- Employment Pass (EP)
- S Pass
- Work Permit
- Personalised Employment Pass (PEP)
- Other applicable work passes

The appropriate pass depends on the worker's role, salary, qualifications,
sector and employer circumstances.

===========================================================
12. EMPLOYMENT PASS
===========================================================

The Employment Pass is intended for foreign professionals, managers,
executives and technicians who meet the eligibility requirements.

The eligibility framework has two main stages.

STAGE 1:
The candidate must earn at least the applicable EP qualifying salary.

STAGE 2:
Unless exempted, the candidate must pass the points-based Complementarity
Assessment Framework (COMPASS).

MOM currently states that the minimum qualifying salary for sectors other
than financial services starts at S$5,600 and increases progressively with
age, up to S$10,700 at age 45 and above.

For financial services, the current minimum starts at S$6,200 and increases
progressively with age.

MOM has published future salary thresholds beginning 1 January 2027.
Therefore, an AI system must check the current MOM source before providing
a salary threshold.

Employers must also meet Fair Consideration Framework job-advertising
requirements before submitting new EP applications, unless an applicable
exception or exemption applies.

OFFICIAL SOURCES:
https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass/eligibility
https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass
https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass/key-facts

===========================================================
13. EMPLOYMENT PASS DOCUMENTS
===========================================================

MOM identifies documents that may be required for EP applications.

Examples include:
- candidate passport/personal particulars page;
- company's latest ACRA business profile or instant information;
- qualification verification where applicable;
- additional documents for certain professions or circumstances.

The exact document list depends on the application.

OFFICIAL SOURCE:
https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass/documents-required

===========================================================
14. S PASS
===========================================================

The S Pass is intended for eligible foreign skilled workers, including
Associate Professionals and Technicians.

MOM currently states that the minimum qualifying salary starts at S$3,300
and increases progressively with age, up to S$4,800 for candidates in their
mid-40s. Financial services candidates have higher requirements.

MOM has announced that the minimum will increase from S$3,300 to S$3,600
for new applications from 1 January 2027 and renewals expiring from
1 January 2028.

S Pass hiring is subject to:
- sector-specific quota;
- dependency ratio ceiling;
- monthly foreign-worker levy;
- applicable employment conditions.

MOM states that since 1 September 2025 the S Pass levy rate has been
harmonised to S$650 across sectors and levy tiers.

OFFICIAL SOURCES:
https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/s-pass/key-facts
https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/s-pass/quota-and-levy/levy-and-quota-requirements

===========================================================
15. S PASS QUOTA
===========================================================

The number of foreign workers an employer can hire is restricted by
sector-specific quotas.

MOM currently lists dependency ratio ceilings including:
- Construction: 83.3%
- Process: 83.3%
- Marine shipyard: 75%
- Manufacturing: 60%
- Services: 35%

The actual number of S Pass holders is also affected by applicable
sub-quotas and workforce composition.

This information is highly relevant to a startup that wants to hire many
foreign skilled workers.

Quota and levy information should be treated as time-sensitive.

OFFICIAL SOURCE:
https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/s-pass/quota-and-levy/levy-and-quota-requirements

===========================================================
16. PERSONALISED EMPLOYMENT PASS
===========================================================

The Personalised Employment Pass is intended for eligible high-earning
foreign professionals and provides greater job flexibility.

MOM currently states a minimum fixed monthly salary of S$22,500 for PEP
eligibility.

The PEP can be issued for up to three years and is not renewable.

PEP holders have job flexibility but professional registration requirements
for regulated professions still apply.

OFFICIAL SOURCES:
https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/personalised-employment-pass/eligibility
https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/personalised-employment-pass/key-facts

17. DATA PRIVACY - PDPA
===========================================================

Singapore's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) establishes a framework
for protecting personal data.

The PDPA covers personal data stored in electronic and non-electronic forms,
subject to its scope and exclusions.

Organizations that collect, use or disclose personal data need to comply
with applicable data protection obligations.

KEY OBLIGATIONS:

1. ACCOUNTABILITY
Organizations should take measures to meet PDPA obligations, including
making information about data protection policies, practices and complaints
processes available where required and designating a Data Protection Officer
(DPO) with publicly available business contact information.

2. NOTIFICATION
Organizations should notify individuals of purposes for which personal data
is intended to be collected, used or disclosed.

3. CONSENT
Organizations generally collect, use or disclose personal data for purposes
for which an individual has given consent, subject to applicable exceptions.

4. PURPOSE LIMITATION
Personal data should only be collected, used or disclosed for appropriate
purposes and in accordance with applicable requirements.

5. ACCURACY
Reasonable effort should be made to ensure personal data is accurate and
complete, especially where it is likely to affect an individual or be
disclosed to another organization.

6. PROTECTION
Organizations must make reasonable security arrangements to protect
personal data from unauthorized access, collection, use, disclosure and
similar risks.

Other PDPA obligations may apply depending on the organization's activities,
data processing, transfers and circumstances.

OFFICIAL SOURCES:
https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/overview-of-pdpa/the-legislation/personal-data-protection-act
https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/overview-of-pdpa/the-legislation/personal-data-protection-act/data-protection-obligations

18. LICENSES AND SECTOR-SPECIFIC COMPLIANCE
===========================================================

Company registration does not automatically authorize every type of
business activity.

Depending on the industry, a company may require additional licences,
approvals, registrations or professional permissions.

Potentially regulated areas can include financial services, healthcare,
food, education, transport, telecommunications and regulated professions.

The AI advisor should therefore ask for:
- industry;
- exact business activity;
- products/services;
- whether the company handles regulated data;
- whether the company employs foreign workers.

The system should then identify relevant licensing or regulatory requirements
from the appropriate Singapore authority.

Do not state that company registration alone gives permission to operate a
regulated activity.

22. RECOMMENDED RAG BEHAVIOUR
===========================================================

The RAG system should:

1. Prefer official Singapore government information.
2. Retrieve the relevant section before generating an answer.
3. Never invent a regulation, fee, threshold, quota or deadline.
4. Mention when information is time-sensitive.
5. Provide source document/section and source URL.
6. Say when the knowledge base does not contain enough information.
7. Clearly distinguish a legal requirement from an AI recommendation.
8. Avoid presenting itself as a lawyer, tax adviser or immigration adviser.
9. For high-impact decisions, recommend verification with the relevant
   government authority.
10. When comparing Singapore with another country, retrieve equivalent
    official information for the other country instead of assuming the
    rules are comparable.
